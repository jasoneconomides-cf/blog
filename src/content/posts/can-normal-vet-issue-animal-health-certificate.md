**Your normal vet can issue an Animal Health Certificate if an Official Veterinarian at the practice is authorised to sign it.** Not every vet has that authorisation, so ask the practice specifically whether it issues Animal Health Certificates for pet travel before booking the appointment.

If your usual practice cannot issue one, GOV.UK says to ask it to help you find an Official Veterinarian, often shortened to OV.

This distinction matters because an ordinary veterinary appointment and an Animal Health Certificate appointment are not automatically the same thing. Your practice may provide your pet's routine care and rabies vaccination but use a particular vet, clinic or appointment process for travel documents.

This article covers journeys from Great Britain—England, Scotland and Wales—to the EU. Northern Ireland and non-EU destinations can involve different documents and procedures. Always confirm the current rules for your complete route.

## What is an Official Veterinarian?

An Official Veterinarian is a vet authorised to perform particular work on behalf of the government. For an Animal Health Certificate, the important point is that the certificate needs to be signed by an OV who can issue that document.

The authorised vet does not have to be a stranger to your pet. They might be:

- your pet's usual vet
- another vet at your normal practice
- a vet who visits or works across several branches
- an OV at a different local clinic
- a specialist pet-travel provider

The practice name alone does not answer the question. Ask whether the person signing the certificate is authorised for Animal Health Certificate work and whether that person will be available for your required dates.

## The question to ask when you call

Instead of asking only, “Do you do travel paperwork?”, ask:

> “Do you have an Official Veterinarian who can issue an Animal Health Certificate for my pet's journey to the EU?”

Then give the practice enough information to identify the right appointment:

- whether you are travelling with a dog, cat or ferret
- how many pets are travelling
- your departure date
- the date and country where you expect to enter the EU
- every transit country on the route
- whether the pet's rabies vaccination is current
- whether the practice already holds the microchip and vaccination records

If you are driving to Spain through France, for example, France is normally the first EU entry point even though Spain is the final destination. Give the practice the full route rather than only the name of your hotel destination.

## Will your pet need to attend the appointment?

Yes. GOV.UK says you must take your pet to the vet to get an Animal Health Certificate.

The vet needs to identify the animal and confirm that the supporting records correspond to that pet. A previous appointment, a photograph of the pet or an emailed vaccination record does not remove the need to bring the animal to the AHC appointment.

Ask the practice what else it wants in advance. GOV.UK says you will need proof of your pet's:

- microchipping date
- vaccination history

Sending readable copies before the visit can give the practice time to identify missing information. Bring any originals the practice requests as well.

## What the vet will need to check

An AHC records information including your pet's identity, microchip and rabies vaccination. The records need to agree with one another.

Before the appointment, check that you can locate:

- the complete microchip number
- the date the microchip was implanted or first read
- your pet's date of birth
- the rabies vaccination date
- the vaccine manufacturer and product name
- the batch number
- the date the vaccination became valid
- the date the vaccination remains valid until

For EU travel, the pet must have been microchipped before—or at the same time as—the rabies vaccination being relied upon. If the vaccination happened first, GOV.UK says the pet will need to be vaccinated again after the microchip requirement is satisfied.

After a first rabies vaccination, you must wait for the vaccine to work before travel. This is at least 21 full days, but GOV.UK advises asking the vet because some vaccines take longer. A valid booster without a break in cover does not normally restart that waiting period.

## When should you contact the practice?

Contact the practice well before the certificate needs to be issued. Early contact lets you:

- confirm that an OV is available
- have the records reviewed
- arrange a rabies vaccination or booster if needed
- understand the practice's fee and appointment process
- reserve an appointment inside the correct travel window

Contacting the practice early does **not** mean the certificate should be issued early.

GOV.UK says an AHC can be used to enter the EU within 10 days after it is issued, with the issue date counted as day one. Work backwards from the date you expect to enter the EU—not merely the date you leave home.

If your pet needs its first rabies vaccination, allow for the applicable vaccine waiting period before the certificate appointment and travel.

## What if your normal vet cannot issue an AHC?

Do not assume that you must move all your pet's routine care to another practice. Ask your normal vet to help you find an OV and to explain how the relevant records can be shared.

A practical sequence is:

1. Ask your practice whether another vet or branch can issue the certificate.
2. Ask whether it can recommend a local OV.
3. Request a complete copy of the microchip and rabies vaccination history.
4. Ask the issuing practice which original records it requires.
5. Confirm the appointment date against your first EU entry date.

When transferring records, compare the microchip number and vaccination dates yourself. Do not rely on a shortened summary if the issuing vet has asked for the full clinical record or original vaccination evidence.

## Does every destination use an AHC?

No. An Animal Health Certificate is not a universal document for every country described as “European”.

For travel to a non-EU country, GOV.UK says you will usually need the destination-specific Export Health Certificate process instead. That also involves nominating an official vet, but it is not the same document or application.

Northern Ireland has separate pet-travel arrangements. Check the dedicated official guidance rather than assuming that a Great Britain-to-France checklist applies.

## Ask for the price in writing

GOV.UK does not set one national price for an Animal Health Certificate. Practices can structure their charges differently, particularly where there are several pets, records need reviewing, vaccinations are required or another treatment is provided.

Ask the practice to explain what its quote includes. Useful questions include:

- Is the certificate appointment included?
- Is record checking included?
- Is the price different for additional pets on the same certificate?
- Are vaccinations or other treatments charged separately?
- What happens if the itinerary changes before issue?

GOV.UK says up to five pets can be added to one AHC. That does not guarantee one fixed fee, but it means you should not assume automatically that every pet needs a separate certificate.

## A current certificate-template detail for 2026

GOV.UK introduced a new Animal Health Certificate template in 2026. Its carrier guidance states that the old template can still be issued by an OV in Great Britain until **30 September 2026**. From **1 October 2026**, the new template must be used.

This is primarily a matter for the issuing OV, not something an owner should try to complete or correct. If your appointment falls around that transition, it is reasonable to ask the practice to confirm that it will use the current template.

Do not alter an official certificate yourself. If you notice something that appears incorrect, ask the issuing OV to advise what must be done.

## Before leaving the AHC appointment

Give yourself time to inspect the completed document with the vet. In particular, compare:

- owner details
- pet description
- microchip number and date
- rabies vaccination details and validity
- destination and route information
- the OV's signature, stamp and dates

Your task is not to interpret every technical field. It is to spot objective mismatches while the authorised vet is available to address them.

If you are travelling with a dog, also plan the return journey. Dogs entering Great Britain from most countries need veterinary tapeworm treatment within a precise window. Our [dog tapeworm-treatment guide](/posts/dog-tapeworm-treatment-returning-great-britain/) explains the 24-to-120-hour rule and exemptions.

## Quick answer checklist

- Your normal vet **may** issue an AHC.
- The certificate must be signed by an authorised Official Veterinarian.
- Ask specifically whether the practice has an OV who issues AHCs.
- Bring your pet and the requested microchip and vaccination evidence.
- Contact the practice early, but issue the certificate within the EU-entry window.
- If the practice cannot issue it, ask for help finding an OV and transferring the records.
- Confirm that an AHC is the correct document for the complete route.
