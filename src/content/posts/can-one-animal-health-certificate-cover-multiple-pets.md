Yes. **One Animal Health Certificate can include up to five pets** travelling from Great Britain to the EU, provided they are dogs, cats or ferrets travelling as part of the same qualifying non-commercial journey.

That does not mean the animals share one set of health details. Each pet must be individually identified on the certificate and must meet the applicable microchip, rabies-vaccination and destination requirements.

For a family taking two dogs to France together, for example, the vet can normally list both dogs on one AHC. You do not automatically need a separate certificate for each dog.

## How many pets can go on one AHC?

GOV.UK states that you can add **up to five pets** to an Animal Health Certificate. For this purpose, the standard EU pet-travel system covers dogs, cats and ferrets.

The limit is about the number of animals covered by the non-commercial pet-travel movement—not a promise that every carrier will accept five animals in one vehicle, cabin or booking. Ferry, rail and airline operators can impose their own capacity, kennel, carrier and booking conditions.

Always check both sets of requirements:

1. the official entry rules for the animals; and
2. the transport operator's rules for your route and vehicle.

Our [Animal Health Certificate guide](/posts/animal-health-certificate-guide/) explains what an AHC is and when it is normally needed.

## Do all the pets need their own health records?

Yes. Putting several pets on one certificate does not allow one pet's details to stand in for another's.

For each animal, the Official Veterinarian will need the relevant evidence, including:

- its microchip number and microchipping date
- its rabies-vaccination history
- confirmation that the microchip was implanted or read before the vaccination relied upon
- any treatment or declaration required for the planned destination

Every microchip number and record should match the correct animal. Check the completed certificate carefully before leaving the appointment, especially when pets have similar names, breeds or vaccination dates.

If you are unsure about the order of events, read [why the microchip must come before the rabies vaccination](/posts/pet-microchip-before-rabies-vaccination/).

## Must the pets travel together?

The pets listed on the certificate should be part of the same non-commercial movement. In practical terms, a household taking its animals on the same outward journey can normally use one AHC containing all of them.

Do not assume one certificate can be divided between unrelated journeys. If one dog leaves on Tuesday and another leaves weeks later, the later trip is not simply an unused portion of the first journey's certificate.

Before booking, tell the vet:

- how many pets are travelling
- who owns them
- who will accompany them
- the date and country of first EU entry
- whether they will all use the same route
- whether any pet will travel separately

Those details let the Official Veterinarian decide how the movement should be documented.

## What if someone else accompanies the pets?

EU rules allow an authorised person to accompany a pet on the owner's behalf in certain circumstances. The owner must provide written authorisation, and the owner's own journey must normally take place within five days of the pet's movement.

The movement must remain non-commercial: it must not be intended to sell the pets or transfer their ownership. If the timing, responsible person or purpose falls outside those conditions, different animal-health rules may apply.

Do not rely on an informal text message or assume a family relationship is enough. Explain the arrangement to the Official Veterinarian before the certificate is prepared and ask what written authorisation must accompany it.

## Can dogs and cats share the same certificate?

Yes. One AHC can include a mixture of dogs, cats and ferrets, up to the applicable total of five, when they are travelling together under the qualifying conditions.

Species-specific requirements still apply. A dog may need tapeworm treatment for entry into certain destinations or for the return to Great Britain, while a cat on the same certificate does not receive that treatment merely because it is listed beside the dog.

For the journey home, use our guide to the [dog tapeworm-treatment timing window](/posts/dog-tapeworm-treatment-returning-great-britain/). Check the rules for every species and every country on the itinerary.

## What happens if you travel with more than five pets?

More than five dogs, cats or ferrets usually takes the movement outside the standard non-commercial pet-travel limit. An exception can apply where the animals are:

- more than six months old; and
- participating in, or training for, a competition, exhibition or sporting event.

You need evidence of the event, such as registration documentation. Simply owning six pets or splitting them between several certificates does not by itself create an exemption.

Current European Commission guidance also gives examples involving separate vehicles and authorised people, but the facts matter. Anyone planning to move more than five animals should check the arrangement with the relevant authorities and an Official Veterinarian before booking transport.

If the event exception does not apply, the animals may need to travel under the rules used for imports or trade rather than the ordinary pet-travel scheme.

## Does one AHC cover another holiday later?

No. GOV.UK says a new AHC is needed for each separate trip from Great Britain to the EU.

The same certificate can be used for onward travel within the EU during the trip and for return to Great Britain within its period of validity, subject to the animals continuing to meet the applicable rules. That does not turn it into a reusable document for a later departure from Great Britain.

An AHC is currently valid after issue for:

- entry into the EU within 10 days
- onward travel within the EU for up to six months after entry
- re-entry to Great Britain for up to six months

Validity can also be constrained by details recorded for an individual pet, including its rabies vaccination. Confirm the dates on your actual certificate rather than treating the maximum periods as personalised clearance.

## Does adding more pets change the price?

Official rules say how many pets may be listed, but they do not set one universal retail price for an AHC appointment. Veterinary practices decide their own charges and may price additional pets differently because each animal's identity and evidence must be checked.

When requesting a quote, ask for the total price for the exact number of animals. Confirm whether the price includes reviewing vaccination records, correcting paperwork, the appointment and any additional treatment.

## What should you ask the vet before booking?

Use this short checklist:

- Can your Official Veterinarian put all of my pets on one AHC?
- Do you need records for each animal in advance?
- Will every pet need to attend so its microchip can be scanned?
- What is the total charge for the whole group?
- Does my route or destination require additional treatment?
- What changes if another person accompanies one or more pets?
- When can I collect the completed certificate?

Only an appropriately authorised Official Veterinarian can issue the certificate. Our guide explains [whether your normal vet can issue an AHC](/posts/can-normal-vet-issue-animal-health-certificate/).

## A worked example: two dogs and one cat going to France

Suppose one owner plans to drive from England to France with two dogs and one cat. All three animals have valid microchips and qualifying rabies vaccinations, and they will travel together in the same vehicle.

The owner can normally ask an Official Veterinarian to include all three animals on one AHC. The vet records each animal separately within it. The owner enters the EU within the certificate's entry window and keeps the original document for the onward journey and return.

Before returning to Great Britain, the two dogs normally require correctly timed tapeworm treatment unless an exemption applies to the route. The cat does not. The vet abroad records each treated dog's details in the correct place.

This example illustrates the process; it is not confirmation for a particular booking. Travel dates, routes, ownership arrangements and individual health records can change what is required.

## The short answer

One Animal Health Certificate can normally cover up to five dogs, cats or ferrets travelling together on the same qualifying non-commercial trip. Each pet still needs its own correct microchip and rabies records, and any species- or destination-specific treatment.
